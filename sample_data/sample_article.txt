India's Chandrayaan-4 Mission Set for Launch in Early 2027

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) announced on Wednesday that
its ambitious Chandrayaan-4 mission, aimed at bringing back lunar soil
samples to Earth, is on track for launch in the first quarter of 2027.
ISRO Chairman S. Somanath said the mission would involve two separate
launches that dock in lunar orbit, a technically complex maneuver that,
if successful, would make India only the fourth country to achieve
robotic sample-return from the Moon, after the United States, the Soviet
Union, and China.

Speaking at a press briefing in Bengaluru, Somanath said the mission
builds directly on the success of Chandrayaan-3, which in 2023 made
India the first country to land near the Moon's south pole. "We have
learned a great deal from Chandrayaan-3, and Chandrayaan-4 represents
the next major leap in our lunar exploration roadmap," he said.

The mission has an estimated budget of approximately 21 billion rupees
(around $250 million), significantly higher than its predecessor due to
the added complexity of the sample-return architecture. Officials said
the collected lunar samples would be studied for clues about the Moon's
volcanic history and the availability of water ice near the polar
regions, which could be crucial for future crewed missions.

Industry analysts and space enthusiasts welcomed the announcement,
noting that a successful sample-return mission would strengthen India's
position in the increasingly competitive global space race, alongside
NASA's Artemis program and China's Chang'e missions. However, some
experts cautioned that the docking maneuver in lunar orbit has never
been attempted by ISRO before and carries significant technical risk.

The Indian government has also hinted at long-term ambitions of setting
up an Indian space station by 2035 and landing an Indian astronaut on
the Moon by 2040, with Chandrayaan-4 seen as a critical technology
demonstrator for those future goals.
